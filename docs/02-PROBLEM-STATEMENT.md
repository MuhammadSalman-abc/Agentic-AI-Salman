# Problem Statement and Discovery Plan

## Problem hypothesis

Product managers, analysts, and delivery teams spend time turning business requests into consistent, testable backlog items. Quality may depend on individual experience, and intent may be lost between source requirement and implementation tasks. This is a hypothesis: the source draft's “2–5 days,” “38 hours,” and “40% missing acceptance criteria” were supplied without samples, research notes, or measurement definitions.

## Users and jobs

| Persona | Job | Pain to validate |
|---|---|---|
| Product manager / owner | Turn outcomes into sequenced work. | Repetitive decomposition, gaps, poor traceability. |
| Business analyst | Clarify rules and preserve intent. | Ambiguity surfaces late; artifacts vary. |
| Engineering lead | Receive implementable work and identify dependencies. | Missing constraints and technical unknowns. |
| Developer / QA | Understand behavior and acceptance evidence. | Vague stories and non-testable criteria. |
| Workspace admin | Control membership and integrations. | Unclear access, provider exposure, audit. |

## Discovery questions

- How many requirements are decomposed per team/month, and how long does this take?
- Which tools and hierarchy are authoritative? Which fields are mandatory?
- What fraction requires clarification? How do teams decide completeness?
- What does a useful draft save, and what edits are still required?
- Which source data may be sent to a model provider?
- Which export destination is essential for a first release?

## Validation plan

Interview target users across roles; observe a permissioned real decomposition; collect sanitized representative inputs and accepted outputs; measure current time; label ambiguity and missing criteria; then run a blinded prototype evaluation. An initial 5–8 interviews is a qualitative discovery suggestion, not statistical proof. Record consent, source permissions, and data handling.
